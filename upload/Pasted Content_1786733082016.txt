# 1. PRODUCT MISSION

Build an AI-native operating system for contractor SMEs.

Construction is the first industry vertical. The architecture must ultimately support other contractor/service industries without rewriting the domain core.

The product's primary job is to turn an incoming opportunity/RFQ into a defensible, professional and internally standardized commercial response.

The primary workflow is:

```text
Opportunity / RFQ
    ↓
Scope understanding
    ↓
Scope completeness / ambiguity analysis
    ↓
Estimate
    ↓
Self-perform / subcontract decisions
    ↓
Commercial adjudication
    ↓
Programme
    ↓
BOQ
    ↓
Method Statement
    ↓
JHA
    ↓
Tender / Bid Pack
    ↓
Submission
    ↓
Win / Loss
    ↓
Project
    ↓
Actuals
    ↓
Knowledge / SOP / price / productivity calibration
```

The product must not feel like:

> "Microsoft Office with construction features."

It must feel like:

> "The estimating and operating memory of a contractor, with Office-compatible work surfaces."

GenOffice's document editors are an important substrate, not the product's center of gravity.

# 2. NON-NEGOTIABLE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

The canonical source of truth is NOT an `.xlsx`, `.docx`, `.pptx`, MSP file, or PDF.

The canonical source of truth is the structured contractor domain model.

The central commercial object is:

```text
Estimate
```

not:

```text
BOQ
```

A BOQ is a projection of an estimate.

A programme is a projection of an estimate.

A Method Statement is a projection of execution knowledge associated with the estimate.

A JHA is a projection of work definitions + hazard controls.

A tender package is a projection of the bid and its approved deliverables.

The architecture must preserve this distinction everywhere.

# 27. BOQ → PROGRAMME GENERATION


# 43. OFFLINE SUPPORT

Construction environments may have poor connectivity.

The product should support offline work where practical.


Initial implementation may limit offline functionality to already-synced project data and non-conflicting operations.

# 76. SUCCESS CRITERIA

The MVP is successful only if a real SME can take a real RFQ and materially reduce the work required to produce a professional bid.

Specifically:

### Estimating

The system can reconstruct historical bids with explainable pricing lineage.

### Standardization

An approved WorkDefinition can drive cost, productivity, programme, method statement, JHA and QA outputs.

### Ambiguity

The system explicitly surfaces incomplete/ambiguous scope rather than burying it in estimator memory.

### Subcontracting

The system exposes uncovered scope in subcontractor quotations.

### Documents

The system produces usable Office-compatible tender deliverables.

### Programme

The system generates a useful first draft of the Programme from the estimate.

### Knowledge

The organization accumulates reusable pricing, productivity and SOP knowledge.

### AI

AI accelerates these workflows without becoming the authority for financial truth.

# 82. FINAL NORTH STAR

Build this product so that, after sufficient use, the company can answer:

> What work are we being asked to do?

> What don't we know yet?

> How have we priced this work before?

> Why is this rate what it is?

> Should we self-perform or subcontract it?

> What does the subcontractor actually cover?

> What assumptions are protecting us commercially?

> How long should this work take?

> What is the expected programme?

> What method and hazards apply?

> What should go into the tender pack?

> Why did we win or lose?

> How did we actually perform?

> What should we change in our standard price or SOP?

And the system should progressively answer those questions from the company's own accumulated knowledge.

That is the product.

Build the system around that loop, not around the office editors.
